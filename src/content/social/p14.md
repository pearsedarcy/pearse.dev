I picked UMAP to lay out my knowledge graph. Then I measured it.

The graph is a 3D view of the Hub's second brain, a small Markdown vault of business ideas. Each concept is embedded with a local model, and its rest position comes from projecting that embedding down to three dimensions, so ideas that mean similar things sit near each other even when nothing links them.

Before: UMAP, because that's what you use for embeddings.

Then I checked how well each layout kept similar concepts close together, on the ten-concept seed vault. PCA, a plain numpy SVD, scored far better. With so few points, UMAP's neighbour graph is mostly noise, and it spread everything evenly over the sphere. That is the one thing a layout by meaning must not do.

After: PCA below 20 concepts, UMAP above. And if the local embedding model isn't reachable, the graph falls back to a plain force layout with a banner instead of failing.

The fancy method is tuned for the data size it was designed for. Measure it at yours.
