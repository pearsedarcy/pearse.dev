There are about 50 architecture decision records across the three platforms I build. Most are under a page.

How I write them:

One decision per record. The title is the decision, not the topic. "No email sends without human approval" beats "Email strategy".

Context in a paragraph. What forced the decision now.

The alternative I rejected, and why. This is the part future me actually reads. Without it, every ADR looks obvious and every reversal looks reasonable.

Consequences, including the bad ones. An append-only time ledger means corrections are new entries, not edits. That's more rows and a slightly odder UI. Written down, so nobody "fixes" it later.

Written before the code, or at the latest in the same PR.

Why bother on a team of one? Because I'm not really a team of one. I work with AI coding agents, and an agent has no memory of why the schema looks the way it does. The ADRs are how it finds out. A decision that isn't written down gets quietly re-made by the next session that touches the code.
