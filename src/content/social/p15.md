Erase a person, and the next sync job can quietly bring them back.

Our Django operations platform anonymises a contact on a GDPR erasure request: name, email, phone, address and the rest, scrubbed in place where bookings or certificates still point at the record.

The platform also reconciles orders from the company's web shop, keyed on email. After an anonymisation, the old address no longer matched anyone. So the reconcile did what it was built to do: it created a new contact, in full, and moved the retained booking onto the copy. [VERIFY: caught in testing or after a real erasure? Copy assumes testing.]

How we handle it now:

The erased address is retired in the same transaction as the anonymisation. A tombstone, not a contact, and it keeps only a hash of the address.

Every import checks tombstones before creating anyone. A new order from that address is parked without its payload.

A member of staff re-adding the person by hand still wins. The tombstone stops machines, not people.

Tests cover the round trip: erase, reconcile, assert nobody came back.

Erasure is not one delete. It's every copy of a person, including the copy some job will make tomorrow from data you don't own.
